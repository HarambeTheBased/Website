English-Speaking Lawyer to Buy a House in Almería, Spain

Updated 22 July 2026 · Antonio Gaitán Abogados · Adra, El Ejido, Roquetas de Mar · Almería

Buying a home on the Almería coast should be exciting, not stressful. But Spanish conveyancing works very differently from the UK, Germany or the Netherlands — and a mistake can be expensive. This guide explains, in plain English, what an independent lawyer does for you, why it matters, and what it costs when buying property in Adra, El Ejido, Roquetas de Mar or anywhere in the province of Almería.

📜 The key point: in Spain, unpaid debts attached to a property — such as outstanding IBI (council tax) or community fees — can pass to the new owner. An independent lawyer working only for you (not the seller or the agency) protects you from inheriting someone else's problems.

1. Why you need an independent lawyer in Spain

In many countries the notary and the estate agent handle much of the buyer's protection. In Spain, that is not the case. The Spanish notary confirms the identity of the parties and that the deed is correctly executed, but does not investigate whether the property has hidden debts, planning problems or boundary discrepancies. The estate agent works for the seller. Nobody checks the purchase on your behalf unless you appoint your own lawyer.

Your independent lawyer

  • Verifies it is debt-free and charge-free
  • Checks planning and licensing compliance

2. What the service covers, step by step

A complete purchase management service should cover every one of these checks. This is exactly what we do for our international clients:

Full conveyancing service

  • Review of all property documentation title deed, energy performance certificate, latest IBI (council-tax) receipt and community-fee status.
  • Land Registry check (nota simple) cross-checked against the Cadastre: owner, boundaries, surface area and legal description.
  • Verification the property is free of charges mortgages, liens, seizures (embargos) and easements, plus any outstanding IBI or community debts that can attach to the property itself.
  • Planning and legal compliance occupancy licence (licencia de ocupación), absence of urban-planning infringements or disciplinary proceedings, and consistency between the physical property and its registered description.
  • Deposit contract (contrato de arras) drafting or review of the private purchase contract that secures the property before completion.
  • Coordination of the public deed (escritura) liaison with the notary and full assistance through to signing and completion day.

📌 Why the nota simple matters: this single Land Registry document reveals who legally owns the property, whether there is a mortgage or seizure on it, and its exact registered description. We can request it in advance, before you commit, so you know the legal position before making any decision.

3. The NIE: your Spanish tax number

To buy property in Spain you need an NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero), a foreigner's identification and tax number. You cannot sign the deed or pay the purchase taxes without it. We can obtain your NIE for you, either in person or under power of attorney, so this never becomes a bottleneck on completion day.

Related steps we can also handle

Opening a Spanish bank account, tax settlement (ITP transfer tax), registration of the deed at the Land Registry, and arranging utility contracts. A single point of contact for the entire process, in English.

4. Costs and taxes: what you will really pay

Beyond the purchase price, a buyer in Andalusia should budget roughly 10–13% in taxes and costs for a resale property. Here is the breakdown:

ConceptApprox. cost
ITP transfer tax (resale property, Andalusia)7% of price*
Notary fees (escritura)€600–1,200
Land Registry fees€400–800
Legal fees (our service)1% + VAT
Gestoría (admin), if used€300–500

⚠ *The cadastral reference value trap. Since 2022, ITP is calculated on the higher of the purchase price or the property's official valor de referencia catastral (cadastral reference value). If you negotiate a price below that reference value, you still pay 7% on the higher figure. We always verify this before the contract is signed, so there are no surprises at the notary.

For a resale home in Andalusia, ITP is a flat 7%. A reduced 6% rate may apply where the property is under €150,000 and will be the buyer's main residence. New-build properties are different: they pay 10% VAT (IVA) plus stamp duty (AJD) instead of ITP.

6. Buying from abroad: power of attorney

You do not need to be in Spain for every step. If you cannot travel for completion, we can act under a power of attorney (poder notarial) and sign the deed on your behalf. The power of attorney can be granted before a Spanish notary, at a Spanish consulate abroad, or before a foreign notary with the Hague Apostille.

🌎 This is how many of our German, British, Dutch and Scandinavian clients complete their purchase: they view the property, agree the terms, grant a power of attorney, and we handle the signing and registration while they are back home.

Frequently asked questions

Do I really need a lawyer to buy property in Spain?
Legally, it is not compulsory — but it is strongly advisable. Unlike the UK or Germany, the Spanish notary does not check for hidden debts, planning breaches or boundary problems, and the estate agent represents the seller. An independent lawyer is the only party investigating the purchase in your interest. Given that unpaid IBI and community debts can pass to the new owner, the protection is well worth the fee.
How much are your fees for handling a property purchase?
Our fee for full management of the purchase is 1% of the purchase price + VAT. Because it is a percentage, you know exactly what it will be as soon as the price is agreed, and we confirm it in writing before starting. It is separate from third-party costs (notary, Land Registry, ITP transfer tax and any gestoría), which are paid directly to each provider — we give you a full written estimate of those too.
What is ITP and how much will I pay in Almería?
ITP (Impuesto sobre Transmisiones Patrimoniales) is the transfer tax on resale property. In Andalusia — which includes the whole province of Almería — it is a flat 7% of the taxable value. A reduced 6% may apply for a main residence under €150,000. Important: since 2022 the tax is calculated on the higher of the purchase price or the cadastral reference value, and it must be paid within 30 days of signing the deed.
Can you handle everything if I cannot travel to Spain?
Yes. With a power of attorney (poder notarial) we can sign the deed and complete the purchase on your behalf. It can be granted before a Spanish notary, at a Spanish consulate in your country, or before a local notary with the Hague Apostille. Many of our international clients complete their purchase remotely this way.
What is an NIE and how do I get one?
The NIE is your Spanish foreigner's identification and tax number. You need it to sign the deed and pay the purchase taxes. We can obtain it for you, in person or under power of attorney, so it never delays completion. It is a one-off number that remains valid for all your future dealings in Spain.
Which areas of Almería do you cover?
We assist buyers across the entire province: Adra, El Ejido, Berja, Roquetas de Mar, Almería city, Vera, Mojacar, and the surrounding coast and countryside. Our office is in Adra, on the western Costa de Almería. We work in English throughout the process.
